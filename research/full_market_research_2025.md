# Sentinel: Comprehensive Market Research (April 2026)

Hex-level (~36km2) conflict risk predictions, 72-hour lookahead, 15-minute refresh, 70+ data sources.

---

## 1. Supply Chain Risk Management (SCRM)

**TAM:** $3.5-12B (2025), depending on scope definition. Most credible estimates cluster around $4-5B for pure SCRM software. The broader "supply chain resilience" market reaches $82.9B by 2035.

**CAGR:** 10-15% (consensus ~12%)

**Average Deal Size:** Enterprise contracts $100K-500K/year. Resilinc and Everstream target Fortune 500 procurement teams. Self-serve tiers emerging but rare.

**Sales Complexity:** HIGH. Enterprise sales cycles 6-12 months. Requires integration with existing ERP/procurement systems. POCs and pilots are standard.

**Barrier to Entry:** MODERATE-HIGH. Customers expect deep supply chain mapping, not just risk scores. However, a focused "geopolitical risk layer" that plugs into existing platforms via API is a viable wedge.

**Key Players:**
- **Resilinc** -- $42.5M revenue (2024), raised $32M total. Focus: sub-tier supplier mapping + risk monitoring.
- **Everstream Analytics** -- ~$78M revenue, raised $74M (Series B $50M in 2023). Focus: AI-driven supply chain risk analytics.
- **Interos** -- $1B valuation, raised $224M. Focus: relationship mapping + risk scoring for government and enterprise.
- **Prewave** -- raised $98M total (Series B Apr 2025). Focus: EU supply chain due diligence / CSDDD compliance.

**Verdict for Student Startup:** NOT first market. Enterprise sales cycles are too long and integration requirements too heavy. However, selling a "geopolitical risk feed" to these platforms as a data supplier is viable once you have traction.

## 2. Marine/Shipping Risk Intelligence

**TAM:** Global marine insurance market $35B (2024), growing to $45.7B by 2033 (3% CAGR). War risk premiums alone approaching $1B/year. Maritime risk intelligence services are a ~$500M-1B niche within this.

**CAGR:** War risk premiums surged 20-50% in 2025 due to Red Sea/Houthi crisis. Overall marine insurance growing 3%, but the risk intelligence/analytics layer growing 15-20%.

**Average Deal Size:** $50K-200K/year for intelligence subscriptions. Shipping companies, insurers, and commodity traders are buyers. War risk premiums can be 0.5-1% of hull value per transit.

**Sales Complexity:** MODERATE. Smaller buyer universe (shipping companies, P&I clubs, Lloyd's syndicates). Relationships matter but market is receptive to new data.

**Barrier to Entry:** MODERATE. Must prove credibility with maritime domain expertise. AIS data integration is table stakes. The hex-level conflict prediction is genuinely novel here.

**Key Players:**
- **Windward AI** -- acquired by FTV Capital for $270M (Mar 2025). Revenue ~$33M TTM. AI-powered maritime risk for insurers, shipping cos, governments.
- **Dryad Global** -- private, acquired IMSA Global (2022). Maritime risk intelligence + cyber. Niche player.
- **Ambrey** -- acquired by Ocean Infinity (2021). ~$123M estimated revenue. Maritime security + risk management.
- **MarineTraffic** -- ~$14.4M estimated revenue, 100+ employees. AIS vessel tracking. Data platform model.
- **CorridorRisk** -- small startup, real-time risk scoring for 7 shipping corridors. Closest competitor to what Sentinel could offer.

**Verdict for Student Startup:** STRONG OPPORTUNITY. Maritime insurers and shipping companies need granular, real-time risk data for route planning and premium pricing. The Red Sea crisis created urgent demand. API-first approach works. CorridorRisk proves a small team can compete. Windward's $270M exit validates the space.

## 3. Travel Risk Management

**TAM:** $3.5-9.6B (2025), depending on definition. Core travel risk management services ~$4B.

**CAGR:** 7-9%

**Average Deal Size:** Enterprise contracts $50K-500K/year. International SOS charges per-employee-per-year (estimated $5-15/employee/year for large enterprises). Smaller corporate plans $5K-50K/year.

**Sales Complexity:** HIGH. Dominated by International SOS ($2.5B revenue, 13K employees). ISO 31030 compliance creates regulatory pull but procurement is slow. Need security clearances and medical response capability that Sentinel doesn't have.

**Barrier to Entry:** HIGH. International SOS and WorldAware (now Crisis24/GardaWorld) have massive moats: 1,200+ locations, medical evacuation capability, 24/7 response centers. A data startup cannot replicate this.

**Key Players:**
- **International SOS** -- $2.5B revenue, 13K employees. Dominant market leader.
- **Crisis24 (GardaWorld)** -- acquired WorldAware, Global Guardian. Physical security + intelligence.
- **Everbridge** -- mass notification + travel risk. Public company.

**Verdict for Student Startup:** NOT viable as primary market. Cannot compete with physical response capability. However, selling risk data feeds TO travel risk companies is a B2B2B opportunity worth exploring later.

## 4. Commodity Trading / Alternative Data

**TAM:** Alternative data market $4.6-14B (2025), growing to $14-21B by 2027. Geopolitical alt data is a subset, estimated $500M-1B.

**CAGR:** 25-44% (depending on source; consensus ~30% for alt data broadly)

**Average Deal Size:** Premium alternative data subscriptions start in "high six figures" ($300K-$1M+/year) for institutional buyers. Smaller quant funds pay $50K-200K/year. API access for individual datasets $10K-100K/year.

**Sales Complexity:** MODERATE. Hedge funds and commodity desks make fast decisions if the signal has alpha. Trial periods are standard (1-3 months). Need to prove signal quality with backtests. Bloomberg and Refinitiv data marketplaces provide distribution.

**Barrier to Entry:** MODERATE. Quant funds care about signal quality, not brand. A 2-person team with a demonstrably predictive signal CAN sell. Distribution via data marketplaces (Bloomberg Enterprise, Refinitiv, Nasdaq Data Link) reduces go-to-market friction.

**Key Players:**
- **Predata** -- acquired by FiscalNote (2021) for undisclosed amount. Raised $5M. Geopolitical predictive intelligence from online activity.
- **GeoQuant** -- acquired by Fitch Group (2022). Raised $8.5M. Country-level political risk scoring for investors.
- **FiscalNote (NOTE)** -- public company, ~$100M revenue, $18M market cap. Owns Predata, Oxford Analytica, Dragonfly. Struggling financially.
- **Verisk Maplecroft** -- subsidiary of Verisk ($14B market cap). 170+ risk indices for 198 countries. Enterprise pricing.

**Verdict for Student Startup:** HIGH POTENTIAL. If Sentinel's 72-hour conflict predictions have demonstrable alpha (correlation with commodity price moves, shipping disruptions, etc.), hedge funds will pay. Start with a backtest paper, distribute through data marketplaces. FiscalNote's struggles suggest the "platform play" is hard but the "signal play" is viable. GeoQuant and Predata both got acquired with tiny teams.

## 5. Insurance / Reinsurance

**TAM:** Political risk insurance capacity ~$4B (2025). Broader political violence insurance growing fast. Parametric insurance market $21B (2025), growing to $24B in 2026 (13.1% CAGR).

**CAGR:** Political risk insurance 5.9%. Parametric insurance 13.1%. Marine war risk premiums volatile but structurally higher.

**Average Deal Size:** Insurers buy analytics platforms for $200K-1M/year. Parametric triggers need certified data feeds -- higher-value contracts. Marine war risk premium decisions affect billions in cargo value.

**Sales Complexity:** HIGH for direct insurer relationships. MODERATE if selling data feeds to existing insurtech platforms or Lloyd's syndicates. Lloyd's Lab accelerator is a viable entry point.

**Barrier to Entry:** MODERATE-HIGH. Insurers are conservative but the market is changing. Only 1% of $130B in Ukraine infrastructure damage was insured -- massive coverage gap. Parametric insurance for conflict is almost nonexistent, creating a greenfield opportunity.

**Key Players:**
- **Munich Re** -- largest reinsurer, active in political violence coverage. Revenue $67B.
- **Swiss Re** -- major reinsurer with political risk products.
- **Lloyd's of London** -- $1.75B in war risk capacity from syndicates. Lloyd's Lab accelerator accepts startups.
- **Beazley, Hiscox, Chaucer** -- specialist Lloyd's syndicates for political risk.

**Verdict for Student Startup:** STRONG LONG-TERM. Not a first market due to sales cycles, but parametric conflict insurance is a massive greenfield. The data layer (conflict probability at hex level as a parametric trigger) is exactly what this market needs and doesn't have. Apply to Lloyd's Lab.

## 6. Government / Defense Intelligence

**TAM:** Defense tech VC funding hit $49.1B in 2025 (up from $27.2B in 2024). US defense budget ~$886B. Intelligence community OSINT spending growing rapidly.

**CAGR:** Defense tech funding growing 50%+ year-over-year.

**Average Deal Size:** SBIR Phase I: $50-250K. SBIR Phase II: $500K-1.5M. OTA contracts: $100K-10M+. Full production contracts: $10M+. Note: SBIR program expired in late 2024 and was not renewed -- major setback for small startups.

**Sales Complexity:** VERY HIGH. Government procurement is notoriously slow (12-24 months). Requires clearances, compliance (FedRAMP, ITAR), and lobbyist-level BD. OTA through DIU is faster but competitive.

**Barrier to Entry:** HIGH. Need security clearances, government-friendly infrastructure, long runway. However, Defense Innovation Unit (DIU) has awarded 500+ OTAs since 2016, with 68% going to small businesses.

**Key Players:**
- **Palantir** -- $250B market cap, ~$3B revenue. Dominant in defense intelligence.
- **Anduril** -- $60B valuation (Mar 2026), raised $4B latest round. Hardware + software defense.
- **Planet Labs** -- satellite imagery for defense. ~$200M revenue.
- **Babel Street** -- OSINT analytics for government. Private.

**Verdict for Student Startup:** NOT NOW. Too slow, too complex, requires clearances. However, winning a small DIU OTA or In-Q-Tel investment would be transformative validation. Revisit after product-market fit elsewhere.

## 7. NGO / Humanitarian

**TAM:** Global humanitarian funding requirements $44.7B (2025) for 186M people across 72 countries. Anticipatory action funding ~$200-500M but stagnating. Humanitarian data/analytics spend is a tiny fraction (~$50-100M).

**CAGR:** NEGATIVE. Humanitarian funding is contracting 34-45% from 2023 levels due to donor fatigue and political shifts. US (largest donor) is cutting aid dramatically.

**Average Deal Size:** $10K-100K for data tools. Often grant-funded, not subscription. Multi-year grants from DFID/BMZ/EU possible at $100K-500K.

**Sales Complexity:** MODERATE but procurement is grant-driven and slow. UN agencies have their own tools (VIEWS, ACLED, HDX). Need to prove value over free alternatives.

**Barrier to Entry:** LOW technically, HIGH commercially. The data is valued but organizations have no budget. Grant cycles are 6-12 months. Academic credibility helps.

**Key Players:**
- **ACLED** -- Armed Conflict Location & Event Data. Nonprofit, ~$10M budget. The standard for conflict data.
- **VIEWS** -- Uppsala University. EU-funded conflict forecasting (1-36 months). Open source. Direct competitor to Sentinel's model.
- **Complex Risk Analytics Fund (CRAF'd)** -- UN-backed, funded by Germany and UK. Invests in risk analytics tools.
- **The Centre for Humanitarian Data (OCHA)** -- operates Humanitarian Data Exchange (HDX).

**Verdict for Student Startup:** CREDIBILITY PLAY, NOT REVENUE. Offer a free tier for humanitarian use. This generates press, academic citations, and moral authority. Revenue comes from commercial markets. Apply for CRAF'd funding if available.

## 8. Media / OSINT

**TAM:** OSINT market $9.8-12.7B (2025). Growing to $90B by 2035. But the "media/OSINT tools" niche (Bellingcat-style) is tiny -- perhaps $100-500M.

**CAGR:** 20-25% for OSINT broadly. The investigative/media segment is grant-funded and slower.

**Average Deal Size:** Media organizations pay $1K-10K/year for tools. OSINT researchers are mostly individual/academic users paying $0-500/year. Enterprise OSINT (Palantir, Babel Street) is $100K-1M+ but that's government, not media.

**Sales Complexity:** LOW for self-serve tools. The OSINT community adopts tools virally. BUT willingness to pay is extremely low.

**Barrier to Entry:** LOW. OSINT researchers love new tools and will promote them on Twitter/X. Bellingcat operates on ~$6.6M/year (mostly grants). This is a community, not a market.

**Key Players:**
- **Bellingcat** -- ~$6.6M revenue (grants), 72 employees. Investigation-focused nonprofit.
- **Maltego** -- OSINT link analysis tool. Enterprise pricing for government/corporate.
- **Dataminr** -- real-time event detection from social media. $1.6B valuation. Government and enterprise.

**Verdict for Student Startup:** EXCELLENT for growth hacking, NOT for revenue. Build a compelling free map, get OSINT Twitter to share it, convert attention into enterprise leads. This is a marketing channel, not a business line.

## 9. Real Estate / Foreign Direct Investment

**TAM:** Global commercial real estate investment ~$1T/year. FDI screening and political risk advisory ~$2-5B. 53% of REITs now use AI-powered geopolitical risk analytics.

**CAGR:** FDI has declined to 1.3% of GDP for top 20 recipients (lowest since 1996), but demand for risk scoring is growing as geopolitics disrupts investment flows.

**Average Deal Size:** Country risk reports $5K-50K. Ongoing risk monitoring $50K-200K/year. Real estate platforms pay $100K+ for data integration.

**Sales Complexity:** HIGH. Real estate investment decisions are relationship-driven. FDI advisory is dominated by Big 4 consulting firms and rating agencies.

**Barrier to Entry:** MODERATE. Country-level risk scoring is commoditized (Maplecroft, EIU, Fitch). Sentinel's hex-level granularity is genuinely differentiated -- investors care about specific sites, not just countries.

**Key Players:**
- **Verisk Maplecroft** -- subsidiary of Verisk ($14B parent). 170+ risk indices, 198 countries. Gold standard for country risk.
- **GeoQuant** -- acquired by Fitch Group (2022). Quantified political risk for investors.
- **BlackRock** -- Geopolitical Risk Dashboard (internal + client-facing). Uses BGRI indicator.
- **EIU (Economist Intelligence Unit)** -- country risk and forecasting. Part of Economist Group.

**Verdict for Student Startup:** LATER STAGE. The hex-level differentiation matters here (a factory in Beirut suburbs vs. one in Jounieh has very different risk), but sales cycles to real estate investors are long. Could be a strong add-on once the data product is proven.

## 10. Cybersecurity / Threat Intelligence

**TAM:** Threat intelligence market $9.3-16.8B (2025), growing to $65B by 2035.

**CAGR:** 15-20%

**Average Deal Size:** Threat intelligence platforms $50K-500K/year for enterprise. Recorded Future charges $100K-300K+/year.

**Sales Complexity:** HIGH. CISOs buy from established vendors. Geopolitical risk is an add-on to cyber threat intelligence, not a standalone purchase.

**Barrier to Entry:** HIGH. The market is dominated by Recorded Future ($250M ARR, acquired by Mastercard for $2.65B), CrowdStrike, Mandiant (Google). Geopolitical risk is ~5-10% of the value proposition.

**Key Players:**
- **Recorded Future** -- $250M ARR. Acquired by Mastercard for $2.65B. 1,900+ clients in 75 countries. Covers cyber, geopolitical, and disinformation.
- **Dataminr** -- $1.6B valuation. Real-time alerts from public data.
- **Flashpoint** -- merged with Risk Based Security. Threat intelligence + geopolitical risk.

**Verdict for Student Startup:** NOT a primary market. Geopolitical risk is a feature inside cyber threat platforms, not a standalone product. Potential data partnership with Recorded Future or Flashpoint, but not a direct market.

## 11. ESG / Sustainability Reporting

**TAM:** ESG software market $1.2-2.2B (2025). Growing to $4-8B by 2033.

**CAGR:** 15-20%

**EU Regulatory Driver:** CSDDD (Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive) affects ~6,000 EU companies + 900 non-EU companies. Compliance deadline July 2029 (reporting by Jan 2030). Requires human rights due diligence across value chains, including conflict zone monitoring.

**Average Deal Size:** ESG platforms $50K-300K/year. Conflict mineral compliance modules $20K-100K/year.

**Sales Complexity:** MODERATE-HIGH. ESG teams are resource-constrained and often buying their first tools. Regulatory mandates create urgency but budgets are still forming.

**Barrier to Entry:** MODERATE. The "conflict zone" angle is unique. Most ESG tools focus on carbon emissions, not geopolitical risk. Sentinel could provide a differentiated data layer for conflict-related due diligence (forced labor in conflict zones, conflict minerals, operational risk).

**Key Players:**
- **Prewave** -- $98M raised. Supply chain risk + ESG due diligence. EU-focused.
- **Sphera** -- Blackstone portfolio company. EHS + ESG + supply chain risk.
- **Sedex** -- ethical trade platform. 85,000+ members.
- **Ecovadis** -- sustainability ratings. $1B+ valuation.

**Verdict for Student Startup:** EMERGING OPPORTUNITY. CSDDD compliance creates a regulatory mandate for conflict zone monitoring in supply chains. Most ESG tools don't cover this well. A "conflict risk data layer" that plugs into existing ESG platforms could work. Timing: 2027-2029 as compliance deadlines approach.

## 12. Prosumer / Developer Tools (Flightradar24 Model)

**TAM:** Difficult to size directly. Flightradar24 demonstrates the model: $41.4M revenue (2024), $500M valuation, 52% profit margins from freemium + API.

**CAGR:** N/A (category-creating)

**Average Deal Size:** Freemium consumer: $0 (ad-supported). Premium subscriptions: $5-50/month. API access: $100-1000/month. Enterprise data feeds: $10K-100K/year.

**Sales Complexity:** VERY LOW for consumer/prosumer. Self-serve, credit card, no sales team needed. Enterprise upsells happen inbound. MarineTraffic (~$14.4M revenue) follows similar model for vessel tracking.

**Barrier to Entry:** LOW. This is the most accessible model for a student startup. Build a compelling public map, add freemium tiers, monetize via API and enterprise data feeds. The "Flightradar24 for conflict zones" pitch is immediately understandable.

**Key Players/Analogues:**
- **Flightradar24** -- $41.4M revenue, $500M valuation, 52% margins. 50,000+ ADS-B receivers. Sold 35% stake to Sprints Capital.
- **MarineTraffic** -- ~$14.4M revenue. Vessel tracking freemium + enterprise data.
- **Liveuamap** -- conflict mapping. Largely volunteer-run, minimal monetization. Proves demand exists.
- **ACLED** -- conflict data provider. ~$10M budget. Academic/NGO focused.

**Verdict for Student Startup:** THIS IS YOUR FIRST MARKET. Self-serve, no enterprise sales needed, viral growth potential via OSINT community and media coverage. The playbook:
1. Free public map (generates press, users, credibility)
2. Premium features (historical data, alerts, detailed analytics) at $10-50/month
3. API access for developers/researchers at $100-500/month
4. Enterprise data feeds for the markets above at $10K-100K/year
5. Use traction to sell into maritime, insurance, alt data, ESG markets
