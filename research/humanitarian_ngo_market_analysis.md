# Humanitarian & NGO Market for Conflict Risk Intelligence

## Executive Summary

The humanitarian sector represents a large, underserved market for conflict risk intelligence. With ~$47B in annual humanitarian appeals, 305M people needing assistance, record aid worker casualties (383 killed in 2024), and increasing duty-of-care legal obligations, demand for better risk analytics is structural and growing. Current tools are fragmented, outdated, or inaccessible. The addressable market for conflict risk intelligence in the humanitarian/NGO space is estimated at **$500M-$1.5B**, sitting at the intersection of travel risk management ($3-9B), humanitarian logistics ($7.6B), and security risk management.

---

## 1. Humanitarian Aid Spending (Market Proxy)

### Global Humanitarian Funding
- **2025 UN appeal:** $47 billion to support 190 million people across 72 countries
- **2024 appeal:** $48.65 billion for 186.5 million people across 71 countries (only 35% funded)
- **2024 actual spending:** International humanitarian assistance fell 11% (~$5B decline), the largest cut ever recorded
- **2025 outlook:** Public funding could contract 34-45% compared to 2023 levels
- **305 million people** globally require humanitarian assistance

### Key Implication
Budget cuts make efficiency tools MORE valuable, not less. Organizations need to do more with less, and risk intelligence directly enables safer, more efficient operations. Donors increasingly scrutinize overhead -- tools that demonstrably save lives and money have strong procurement justification.

## 3. Existing Tools & Competitors

### Free/Public Tools
| Tool | Provider | What It Does | Limitations |
|---|---|---|---|
| **INFORM Risk Index** | JRC/UNDP/OCHA | Country-level risk scoring across 3 dimensions | Annual updates only, country-level (no sub-national), backward-looking |
| **INFORM Severity Index** | ACAPS | Crisis severity ranking | Descriptive not predictive, coarse granularity |
| **INFORM Warning** | JRC | Early warning signals | Limited conflict-specific forecasting |
| **ACAPS** | Independent NGO | Crisis analysis, needs assessments for 150 countries | Free but qualitative, no automated alerts |
| **VIEWS/ViEWS** | Uppsala/PRIO | AI-powered conflict forecasting 1-36 months ahead | Academic tool, limited real-time data, funding challenges for maintenance |
| **GCRI** | EU JRC | Global Conflict Risk Index, 86% sensitivity | Country-level, annual, research-oriented |
| **ReliefWeb** | OCHA | Humanitarian information aggregation | Information portal, not analytics platform |

### Commercial Competitors
| Company | Revenue/Scale | Offering | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| **International SOS** | **$1.4-2.5B revenue**, 9,000+ enterprise clients | Medical/security assistance, travel risk mgmt | Enterprise-focused, expensive, not conflict-prediction focused |
| **Crisis24 (GardaWorld)** | Part of GardaWorld (~$5B CAD revenue) | Largest private-sector analyst team, 200K+ sources in 35+ languages, AI threat detection | Corporate focus, expensive, not humanitarian-tailored |
| **Everbridge** | ~$416M ARR | Critical event management, mass notification, travel risk | Platform play, not conflict-specific, enterprise pricing |
| **RiskPal** | Startup | Digital risk assessment for NGOs, customizable templates | Risk assessment workflow tool, not predictive intelligence |
| **Solace Global** | SME | Travel risk management for NGOs, on-demand security monitoring | Reactive monitoring, not predictive analytics |
| **INSO** | Non-profit | Free safety coordination for 1,200+ NGOs in 17 high-risk countries | Free but limited to specific countries, no predictive modeling |

### Key Insight on Competitive Landscape
The market is bifurcated:
- **Free tools** (INFORM, ACAPS, VIEWS) are too coarse, too slow, or too academic
- **Commercial tools** (International SOS, Crisis24, Everbridge) are priced for Fortune 500 corporates ($50K-$500K+/year), not NGOs
- **No one** offers real-time, hex-level, predictive conflict intelligence at NGO-accessible pricing

## 4. Security Risk Management (SRM) Market for NGOs

### Market Size Estimates
- **Travel Risk Management market:** $3-9B globally (estimates vary; most credible range $3-4B, growing at 7-9% CAGR)
- **Security Risk Management market:** $17.2B in 2025, growing to $35B by 2035 (7.4% CAGR) -- but this is total market, not humanitarian-specific
- **Humanitarian logistics market:** $7.6B in 2025, growing to $13.9B by 2035 (6.2% CAGR)
- **Humanitarian-specific SRM spending:** No clean market figure exists. Estimated at **1-3% of total humanitarian spending** based on GISF research, implying **$350M-$1B annually**

### GISF Research on SRM Costs
The GISF study "The Cost of Security Risk Management for NGOs" found:
- Security costs are systematically under-budgeted
- Security-related costs are often first to be cut during budget reductions
- Donors increasingly accept security costs in program budgets (typically 4-7% overhead allowance)
- No standardized percentage; varies dramatically by context
- GISF developed a Risk Management Expense Portfolio (RMEP) tool to help NGOs estimate security costs

### UN Security Spending
- **UN Peacekeeping budget:** $5.6B (FY 2024-2025)
- **UNDSS** operates in 131 countries but faces chronic budget pressure
- Security spending is embedded across all UN agency budgets, not centralized

## 5. Duty of Care Requirements Driving Demand

### Legal Framework
- **Definition:** Legal and moral obligation to take reasonable steps to ensure safety/well-being of employees
- **Four pillars:** Duty of Information, Duty of Prevention, Duty of Monitoring, Duty of Intervention
- **France's Loi de Vigilance (2017):** Requires large companies to identify and address human rights/safety risks in operations and supply chains
- **Growing case law:** Directors/executives bear personal legal liability for failing to uphold duty of care
- **Consequences of failure:** Financial penalties, reputational damage, criminal liability

### NGO-Specific Obligations
- Pre-deployment risk assessments now standard practice
- Must know employee locations at all times in conflict zones
- Required to provide security training, evacuation plans, medical access
- Psychological support obligations (PTSD, trauma)
- Insurance/liability coverage requirements

### Why This Matters for Sentinel
Duty of care creates **regulatory demand** for risk intelligence tools. NGOs must demonstrate they conducted proper risk assessments -- a conflict intelligence platform provides auditable, documented risk analysis that satisfies legal requirements. This is a compliance sale, not just an efficiency sale.

## 6. Aid Worker Security Crisis (Demand Driver)

### 2024: Deadliest Year on Record
- **383 aid workers killed** (record high)
- **308 wounded**
- **125 kidnapped**
- **45 detained**
- **599 major attacks** (up from 420 in 2023, a 43% increase)
- **Gaza:** 181 humanitarian workers killed, 194 major attacks
- **Sudan:** 60 killed, 64 major attacks
- State actors were the most common perpetrators (182 killed, 119 wounded)

### Geographic Hotspots
Palestine (194 attacks), Sudan (64), South Sudan (47), Nigeria (31), DRC (27). Violence increased in 21 countries year-over-year.

### Implication
Record casualties create urgent institutional demand for better security intelligence. Every major INGO is re-evaluating their security protocols and tools.

## 7. Pain Points -- Why Current Solutions Are Inadequate

1. **Granularity gap:** INFORM and GCRI operate at country level. Conflict risk varies block-by-block. No commercial tool offers hex-level (H3) resolution
2. **Latency:** Most indices update annually or quarterly. Conflicts escalate in hours
3. **Prediction vs. description:** ACAPS and INFORM describe current conditions. They don't predict where violence will occur next
4. **Price barrier:** International SOS and Crisis24 price for corporate clients ($100K-$500K+/year). NGOs can't afford this
5. **Fragmentation:** Organizations piece together 5-10 different free tools (ACAPS + INFORM + GDELT + Twitter monitoring + local contacts). No unified view
6. **Actionability:** Academic forecasting tools (VIEWS) produce probability maps but don't integrate with operational workflows
7. **Local context:** Commercial tools lack ground-truth data from conflict zones. Sentinel's multi-source fusion (ACLED + GDELT + FIRMS + local Telegram) provides richer context
8. **Accountability gap:** Free tools don't provide audit trails for duty-of-care compliance

## 8. How NGOs Procure Analytics/Technology

### Procurement Channels
1. **Direct procurement:** Large INGOs (ICRC, MSF, Save the Children) have procurement departments and IT budgets
2. **Donor-funded:** Security tools can be written into program budgets (donors increasingly accept 4-7% overhead)
3. **Framework agreements:** FCDO's Conflict, Stability and Security Fund Framework (80 call-offs). USAID procurement processes
4. **Consortium/pooled procurement:** Multiple NGOs share costs through coordination bodies (GISF, INSO)
5. **Innovation grants:** Humanitarian innovation funds (Elrha/HIF, CERF, Grand Challenges)
6. **Free/freemium adoption:** Many NGOs adopt free tools first, then upgrade (ACAPS model)

### Key Procurement Realities
- **Long sales cycles:** 6-18 months for institutional buyers
- **Proof-of-concept required:** Pilots before full procurement
- **Donor approval needed:** Technology costs must be justified in grant proposals
- **Transparency requirements:** Open data/open source preferences in humanitarian sector
- **70% of NGOs** report effective procurement practices significantly enhance operational efficiency
- **Digital transformation** delivers 30-50% operational cost reductions for NGOs that adopt it

### Pricing Strategy Implications
- Freemium model essential (free tier for small NGOs, paid for enterprise features)
- Consortium licensing (GISF's 140 members as a channel)
- Donor co-funding (FCDO, Sida, BMZ as funders of risk tools)
- Per-country or per-mission pricing rather than enterprise-wide

## 9. Emerging Trends

### Anticipatory Action
- **Definition:** Pre-agreed triggers based on forecasts that release pre-arranged financing for pre-agreed activities
- **CERF commitment:** $124.7M pre-committed for anticipatory action frameworks (up from $48M in 2020)
- **2024 CERF disbursement:** $67M for anticipatory/early action for climate shocks
- **Current limitation:** Only 0.2% of humanitarian funding ($78M in 2021) went to anticipatory action
- **Conflict gap:** Most anticipatory action frameworks focus on climate/weather. Conflict anticipatory action is emerging but underdeveloped
- **Sentinel opportunity:** Conflict prediction models that can serve as trigger mechanisms for anticipatory action financing

### Forecast-Based Financing
- WFP, IFRC, FAO leading implementation
- Pre-positioned funding released automatically when forecast thresholds are crossed
- Currently limited to climate hazards (floods, drought)
- Extension to conflict is a major research frontier

### AI/ML Adoption in Humanitarian Sector
- WFP-Palantir deal ($45M/5yr) signals willingness to invest in data analytics
- Palantir now embedded in Gaza aid delivery coordination
- Growing acceptance of AI tools, but with strong data ethics requirements
- 220+ companies engaged in 50+ major humanitarian tech partnerships

### Localization Agenda
- Push to empower local/national NGOs with tools previously only available to large INGOs
- INSO model (free services to 1,200+ NGOs) demonstrates demand for accessible security tools
- Local organizations often have worst access to risk intelligence

## 11. Market Sizing Summary for Sentinel

### Bottom-Up Estimate
- ~50 major humanitarian organizations with >$100M budgets
- ~500 mid-size INGOs with $10-100M budgets
- ~1,200+ NGOs registered with INSO in high-risk contexts
- ~9,000+ organizations use travel risk management services (International SOS client base)

**Pricing tiers:**
- Enterprise (UN agencies, large INGOs): $50-200K/year x 50 orgs = **$2.5-10M**
- Mid-tier (mid-size INGOs): $10-50K/year x 200 orgs = **$2-10M**
- SMB/field (small NGOs, local orgs): $1-5K/year x 1,000 orgs = **$1-5M**
- **Direct humanitarian TAM: $5.5-25M** (conservative, platform revenue only)

### Top-Down Estimate
- Total humanitarian spending: ~$35-47B annually
- Security/risk management: 1-3% = $350M-$1.4B
- Technology/analytics portion: 10-20% of SRM spend = **$35-280M**
- Sentinel addressable (conflict-specific intelligence): **$50-150M**

### Adjacent Market Expansion
- Supply chain risk (from existing business model): $3-5B TAM
- Insurance/parametric triggers: $1-4B TAM
- Alt data finance: $1-2B TAM
- **Humanitarian adds a fourth revenue wedge without cannibalizing the others**

## 12. Strategic Recommendations

1. **Lead with free tier** -- Mirror INSO/ACAPS model. Offer free conflict risk dashboard to build adoption and credibility
2. **Target GISF as channel** -- 140 NGO members, all focused on SRM. Single partnership reaches entire sector
3. **Position for anticipatory action** -- Conflict prediction triggers for forecast-based financing is a blue ocean
4. **Duty of care compliance angle** -- Sell auditable risk assessments, not just maps
5. **Seek donor co-funding** -- FCDO, Sida, EU Horizon Europe all fund conflict early warning research
6. **INSO partnership** -- They serve 1,200+ NGOs but lack predictive analytics. Natural complement
7. **Pilot with OCHA or WFP** -- High-visibility validation, opens all other UN agencies
8. **Consortium licensing** -- Pool pricing across NGO clusters operating in same geography (e.g., all Levant-focused organizations)
