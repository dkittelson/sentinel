# Insurance & Reinsurance Market Opportunity for Sentinel

*Research compiled March 2026*

---

## 1. War Risk Insurance Market (2025-2026)

### Market Size
- Global war risk premiums estimated at ~$1 billion as of January 2025
- Premiums surged 20-50% in 2025 due to Red Sea/Houthi tensions
- The market underwrites ~$150 billion worth of cargo through the Red Sea and Black Sea corridors alone

### Red Sea / Houthi Crisis Premium Impact
- Pre-crisis baseline: ~0.05-0.1% of hull value for standard transits
- Peak 2024 (daily attacks): up to 1% of hull value per 7-day voyage
- Post-ceasefire 2025: fell to ~0.2% from 0.5%, but still 2-4x above pre-crisis levels
- Key insight: **premiums have NOT returned to normal.** Kpler's analysis states the path back to normalcy is "measured in quarters, not weeks" and elevated pricing may become permanent

### Strait of Hormuz Crisis (March 2026) - LIVE
- Following US-Israeli strikes on Iran (Feb 28, 2026), premiums exploded:
  - From 0.25% to 1-7.5% of hull value
  - A $200-300M VLCC now costs $2-15M extra per transit
  - 300%+ surge vs January 2025 levels
  - A large tanker that previously paid ~$100K per transit now pays $400K+
- All 12 International Group P&I Clubs issued 72-hour cancellation notices
- Lloyd's JWC redesignated the entire Arabian Gulf as a conflict zone (JWLA-033, March 3, 2026)
- Daily VLCC charter rates quadrupled to ~$800K/week
- Hapag-Lloyd imposed $3,500/container War Risk Surcharge

### What Lloyd's Syndicates Pay for Conflict Data
- No public pricing found for data vendor contracts
- However, syndicates use "multiple information service providers" including political risk consultants, shadow rating methodologies, and asset valuation services
- Syndicate growth is "based on disciplined underwriting with pricing built on superior data analytics"
- Lloyd's Futureset has modeled a $14.5 trillion 5-year global loss scenario from geopolitical conflict, signaling massive institutional demand for better risk quantification

### Key Implication for Sentinel
War risk is the hottest line in insurance right now. Underwriters are repricing weekly/daily based on geopolitical events, and they are hungry for granular, forward-looking data rather than backward-looking incident reports.

## 2. Parametric Insurance for Conflict

### Current State
- **No one has built a conflict-triggered parametric insurance product yet.** This is a wide-open greenfield.
- Parametric insurance is booming for natural catastrophes (projected $50B+ market by mid-2030s)
- The first dedicated parametric Lloyd's syndicate launched recently
- World Bank has mobilized $5.5B+ in parametric insurance capital for emerging markets
- UK FCDO has trialed parametric insurance for humanitarian contexts

### What Would Be Needed for a Conflict Parametric Trigger
- **Index design**: A verifiable, objective conflict intensity index (Sentinel's hex-level scores could serve this role)
- **Trigger threshold**: Pre-defined score level (e.g., hex score exceeds 0.70 for 72+ hours)
- **Data oracle**: Independent third-party verification of the trigger event
- **Basis risk management**: The trigger must correlate closely with actual losses
- **Historical calibration**: Backtested trigger performance against actual loss events

### Why This Is Sentinel's Biggest Opportunity
Parametric conflict insurance would be a category-creating product. The trigger mechanism is the hardest part — and that is exactly what Sentinel builds. Sentinel could be the "index provider" (like CoreLogic for property cat) rather than the insurer.

### Potential Product Structure
1. Sentinel provides the conflict intensity index
2. Lloyd's syndicate or ILS fund writes the parametric cover
3. Payout triggers when Sentinel's verified score crosses threshold in a defined hex/region
4. Use cases: trade disruption cover, cargo-in-transit, supply chain, NGO operational continuity, infrastructure project force majeure

## 3. Political Risk Insurance (PRI) Market

### Market Size & Growth
- $1.24B in 2024, projected $2.20B by 2034 (CAGR 5.9%)
- Total market capacity: ~$4B in 2025 ($2.23B private insurers + $1.75B Lloyd's syndicates), up from $3.71B in 2024
- Demand projected to rise 33% per Howden's 2025 survey of multinationals ($1B+ revenue)

### Key Players
| Player | Role |
|--------|------|
| **MIGA** (World Bank) | Multilateral guarantor, covers expropriation, war, civil disturbance, breach of contract |
| **Lloyd's of London** | $1.75B capacity; increased line sizes 50% in June 2025 (from $20M to $30M) |
| **AIG** | Strategic investment in specialty operations announced Nov 2025 |
| **Chubb** | Major PRI underwriter |
| **Zurich** | Global PRI capabilities |
| **AXA XL** | Leading market position |

### What PRI Underwriters Use Today
- Country risk ratings (qualitative, updated quarterly at best)
- Political risk consultants with on-the-ground capabilities
- Shadow rating methodologies
- News monitoring services
- MIGA's own country assessments

### What's Missing (Sentinel's Gap to Fill)
- **Granular sub-national risk**: PRI underwriters work at country or city level — hex-level would be transformative
- **Forward-looking prediction**: Current tools are mostly backward-looking or qualitative
- **Real-time updates**: Ratings update quarterly; conflict dynamics shift daily
- **Quantitative scoring**: Most assessments are narrative/qualitative, not machine-readable risk scores
- **72-hour prediction horizon**: No current provider offers short-term tactical forecasting

## 4. Marine War Risk Insurance

### How Premiums Are Set
- Base hull & machinery (H&M) war risk premium is quoted annually
- **Additional Premiums (APs)** are charged per voyage when a vessel enters a JWC Listed Area
- APs are individually negotiated between underwriters and brokers
- Rating shifts weekly or daily based on geopolitical changes
- 7-day renewable policies are standard for high-risk transits

### Key Underwriters
- **Ascot** (Lloyd's)
- **Canopius** (Lloyd's) — operates ShipWRITE platform
- **Hiscox** (Lloyd's)
- **Beazley** (Lloyd's)
- **Norwegian Hull Club**
- **Swedish Club**
- P&I Clubs (International Group of 12)

### JWC Listed Areas Process
- The **Joint War Committee** comprises underwriting reps from Lloyd's and IUA company markets
- Meets quarterly to review high-risk areas
- Works with independent security adviser **Herminius** to assess risk
- Publishes Listed Areas via numbered circulars (latest: JWLA-033, March 2026)
- Factors considered: historical incident data, geopolitical tensions, organized crime presence
- **JWC does NOT set premiums** — only designates areas; pricing is market-driven
- Vessels must notify underwriters before entering Listed Areas
- The 2026 Hormuz crisis expanded listed areas to include Bahrain, Djibouti, Kuwait, Oman, Qatar

### Data Feeds Used by Marine War Risk Underwriters
- **AIS (Automatic Identification System)**: Primary data source — vessel position, speed, direction
  - Providers: Kpler, MarineTraffic, Spire, Windward
  - Used for: pre-bind screening, behavioral analysis, real-time monitoring
- **Behavioral analytics**: AIS transmission gaps, routing anomalies, GNSS manipulation, STS activity
- **Ownership/flag screening**: Sanctions compliance
- **GNSS jamming alerts**: Regional interference detection
- **Security advisories**: Herminius, private intelligence firms
- **News feeds**: Real-time incident monitoring

### Notably Absent from Current Underwriting
- **No systematic use of ACLED or GDELT** in marine war risk underwriting (these are used more in academic/NGO contexts)
- **No predictive conflict scoring** — underwriters react to events, they don't anticipate them
- **No hex-level granularity** — risk is assessed at corridor/area level

## 5. Trade Credit Insurance

### Market Structure
- Coface, Atradius, and Allianz Trade (formerly Euler Hermes) control ~70% of global capacity
- Atradius has credit information on 240M+ companies worldwide
- Market capacity is near all-time highs with multiple carriers

### Political Risk Component
- Trade credit policies include political risk coverage (non-payment due to currency issues, political unrest, expropriation)
- Coface has particular expertise in political risk and emerging markets
- These insurers maintain proprietary country risk scoring systems

### Do They Buy External Conflict Data?
- Not specifically confirmed in public sources, but:
  - They maintain massive internal databases and proprietary scoring
  - Political risk is a component of their country risk models
  - The 2025-2026 tariff/geopolitical environment is driving demand for better risk differentiation
- Trade credit insurers are less likely early customers than specialty war risk or PRI underwriters
- Their budgets for external data are large but their processes are slow and bureaucratic

### What They Would Need from Sentinel
- Country or region-level aggregated scores (not hex-level — too granular for trade credit)
- Historical track record proving predictive value
- Integration with their existing scoring frameworks
- API delivery with SLA guarantees

## 6. What Data Sentinel Needs to Add for Insurance Customers

### Historical Loss Data
- **Critical.** Insurers need to correlate Sentinel's risk scores with actual insured losses
- Partnership with a broker or syndicate to access anonymized loss data would be ideal
- Alternatively: use ACLED event data as a proxy for "loss events" in backtesting

### Actuarial-Grade Calibration
- Scores must be calibrated to real-world event frequencies and severities
- Need to demonstrate: "When Sentinel says 0.70+, X% of the time a qualifying event occurs within Y hours"
- Precision-recall curves, ROC analysis, and Brier scores are the language actuaries speak
- You already have `analyze_pr.py` and backtesting infrastructure — this needs to be formalized into an actuarial validation report

### Specific Reporting Formats
- **Bordereaux format**: Standard insurance data exchange format for exposure/claims reporting
- **ACORD standards**: Industry data interchange standards
- **API delivery**: RESTful API with documented endpoints, SLA, uptime guarantees
- **Audit trail**: Every score must be reproducible and explainable (which features drove it)

### Regulatory Compliance
- **Solvency II** (EU/UK): Three-pillar framework
  - Pillar I: Quantitative capital requirements — if insurers use Sentinel data in internal models, data must meet Article 82 (appropriateness, completeness, accuracy) and Article 121 (accurate, complete, appropriate data for internal models)
  - Pillar II: Governance and risk management documentation
  - Pillar III: Supervisory reporting and public disclosure
  - Independent model validation is required when data feeds into SCR calculations
- **Lloyd's own oversight**: 2025 Market Oversight Plan specifically highlights geopolitical risk; any data vendor feeding syndicate models needs to demonstrate data governance

### Port-Level vs Asset-Level vs Hex-Level
- **Marine war risk**: Needs corridor/route-level scoring (hex is appropriate, maybe aggregate to shipping lanes)
- **PRI**: Needs sub-national scoring (hex is ideal — unique differentiator)
- **Property/terrorism**: Needs asset-level scoring (hex works, but may need to score down to specific coordinates)
- **Trade credit**: Needs country-level aggregation (hex is too granular — aggregate up)
- **Recommendation**: Build an API that serves scores at multiple resolution levels — hex, municipality, corridor, country

### Additional Data Sentinel Should Integrate for Insurance
- **AIS vessel tracking data** (for marine war risk relevance)
- **Infrastructure/asset databases** (ports, refineries, pipelines in the Levant)
- **Sanctions screening overlay** (OFAC, EU, UN lists)
- **Historical insurance loss data** (via broker partnership)
- **Supply chain mapping** (which trade routes pass through which hexes)

## 9. The Big Opportunity: Conflict Risk Index Provider

The most valuable long-term position is **not** selling data feeds to individual syndicates — it's becoming the **reference index** for conflict risk, analogous to:

- **RMS/AIR/CoreLogic** for natural catastrophe risk (each worth $1B+)
- **Moody's/S&P/Fitch** for credit risk
- **MSCI** for ESG risk

If Sentinel's scores become the standard trigger mechanism for parametric conflict insurance, the company would sit at the center of a multi-billion-dollar risk transfer ecosystem. Every parametric policy would reference Sentinel's index, generating per-policy licensing fees.

**This is a venture-scale outcome** if the index gains market adoption, even starting with a single region (Levant).
